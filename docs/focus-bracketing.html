Focus bracketing from the SD card (GR IIIx 1.60)

Goal: a focus bracket for focus stacking, from close-ups (about 12–50 cm) to landscapes, started in the camera without a phone, on the stock firmware. Offline analysis of the decoded 1.60 image, 2026-11-25; nothing has been run on a camera yet. Labels: Read, Structural, Inferred, as in the other documents. The addresses in §3 are checked by tools/focus/focus_probe.py check.

Short answer

  • The camera has no focus bracket: its bracket kinds are exposure, white balance and contrast (Read: StillBracketKind, StillExposureBracketStep).
  • Everything a bracket needs is reachable from an SD-card script on the stock firmware: the shutter (SW_sws/SW_swr, as in the grain sessions), the Snap focus mode and its distance (UserData Set StillFocus Snap, StillSnapDistanceCm), and the camera state through the phone API on 127.0.0.1 (Read; remote control).
  • The shell takes only the menu distances 30, 100, 150, 200, 250, 350 and 500 cm and Infinity. The setter behind it stores any number, and the lens path passes centimetres through to a calculation, not to a table (Read). One instruction rewritten in RAM therefore allows any distance (§3). Whether the lens really goes there is what the probe card finds out (§4).

3. The one-word RAM patch (Read)

AddressStockPatched
0x532de4e4 (the "30" branch)e3a0101e mov r1, #30movw r1, #N, e.g. e300102d for 45 cm (10–10,000 cm)

Only the compare at 0x532de400 branches to this instruction, and only the shell parser calls the matcher, so shooting code does not change. The card writes the word with the multipatch writer of the grain sessions (stock word checked first, cache flushed), sends UserData Set StillSnapDistanceCm 30, and puts the stock word back before the shutter is pressed. A power-off undoes it in any case.

4. The probe card

python3 tools/focus/focus_probe.py card writes a recipe card with one solo run (about 2.5 minutes, one power-on):

  1. It logs /v1/props and /v1/photos from 127.0.0.1: field names, values only for focus, drive and mode fields, and the number of JPGs. Wi-Fi names and keys, serials, addresses and file names are never logged; the filter splits nested objects so each name is judged on its own.
  2. Focus Snap, AF lamp on, then a 20 s slow blink: put the camera on a table or tripod, facing things from about 30 cm to far away.
  3. Twelve frames: 30 cm (stock), 45*, 70*, 100, 120*, 150, 250, 500 cm, Infinity, then 45* and 120* again with a switch Focus Infinity → Snap after the setting (in case the lens only moves on a mode change), then 30 cm again (* = patched). Props are logged once more after the first patched value.
  4. End: snap distance 250 cm and Focus Select (options --snap-after, --focus-after), props and photos again, lamp on for 10 s.

5. How the probe is measured

focus_probe.py import splits each photo into 24 × 16 tiles and takes the mean absolute Laplacian of each. A fit then gives every tile a depth and every patched frame a focus distance, with the stock frames held at their menu distances (Inferred model: sharpness falls off as 1/(1 + (error/w)²) in dioptres, with a floor). The report also gives each frame's similarity to the first 30 cm frame: a patched value that the camera ignored looks the same as 30 cm. Both checks pass on synthetic frames (tools/tests/test_focus_probe.py).

Maker notes of the probe frames

Two Pentax maker-note tags change with the focus (exiftool -u; Read on R0007244–R0007255):

  • 0x0099, first byte: the snap setting, not the lens: 7 for 30 cm, 1 for 100, 2 for 150, 4 for 250, 5 for 500, 255 for Infinity, 0 for every off-list value.
  • 0x0240, second 32-bit word: changes exactly with the frame groups of §5a, with magnitudes like the AF logs' lens positions (about 15,900–16,300): Infinity 16191, 500 cm 16351, 250 cm 16608, 150 cm 16961, then 100 cm 19237–19301 and 30 cm 15774–15806. Inferred: the lens position at capture. From Infinity to 150 cm it rises steadily with 1/distance (about 800–1150 per dioptre), but 100 cm and 30 cm do not fit that line, so the scale is not settled.

5b. ApplySnapDistance and probe 2

ApplySnapDistance (0x532af3d8, Model/FocusModel.cpp; Read) runs in Snap mode (focus code 10; 11 = Infinity sends −1). It reads the stored cm (through 0x532af07c, which turns 30 into 40 in one mode) and maps it in a switch to millimetres: 30 → 300, 40 → 400, 100 → 1000, 150 → 1500, 200 → 2000, 250 → 2500, 350 → 3500, 500 → 5000. It then sends the mm to the lens task (0x532aeb68, message 0x4241080b). Any other cm value reaches an assert ("false") and sends nothing. That fits the probe: after the Infinity → Snap switch, 45 and 120 cm ended at 30 cm (Inferred: the fallback on the failed apply). No direct caller was found (reached through a pointer). AfSnapCount is only a usage counter in the camera log, not a table.

Probe 2 (focus_probe.py card --probe 2, 9 frames) patches the mm of the 30 cm case (0x532af4d4 moveq r1, #300) and of 40 cm (0x532af4f8 mov r1, #400) to the asked mm, sends 30, switches Focus Infinity → Snap, restores the words and shoots: 30 (stock), 45*, 70*, 100, 120*, 150, 20 cm* (closer than the menu), Infinity, 30, every frame with the switch. If the lens takes the sent mm, the patched frames land between their stock neighbours.

Probe 1 also lost its props lines to a busybox awk rule: "name (" is read as a call of a user function. The filter no longer does that (and a test checks it).

6. The bracket planner and card

tools/focus/focus_bracket.py:

  • Plan (Inferred, thin-lens depth of field in dioptres, f = 26.1 mm, circle of confusion 0.02 mm). A frame focused at D is sharp from D − h to D + h with h = N·c/f² (f/5.6: h = 0.164 D, hyperfocal 6.1 m; exiftool gives 6.18 m for these photos). The first frame sits at near − h, the last at far + h, or at the hyperfocal distance when far is infinity. The frames between are spaced evenly in dioptres, with neighbouring zones overlapping by a quarter of a zone. For example, 0.25 m to infinity at f/5.6 takes 16 frames, 1 m to infinity at f/8 takes 3, and 0.2–0.5 m at f/8 takes 9.
  • Card. All seven millimetre loads of ApplySnapDistance (§5b) are rewritten for a round of up to seven frames, so menu value k stands for frame k. Each frame is a UserData Set StillSnapDistanceCm with that menu value, Focus Infinity, Focus Snap, then the shutter. After each round the stock words go back, and the card ends with the settings restored. Probe 2 tested only the 30/40 pair; the other six words follow the same pattern (Read) but have not run.
  • Check. check reads each frame's lens position (maker note 0x0240), fits a line over 1/distance and flags frames off it by more than 0.08 D. On probe 2's six frames from 30 to 150 cm: 1123 per dioptre, no frame off the line.
  • Pace (estimate): about 5 s per frame. That is the Focus switch (2 × 0.8 s), a 0.6 s half-press without AF and 1.5 s after the shot (--save-ms).

First bracket (stock 1.60, 2026-11-25, R0007265–R0007280)

0.25 m to infinity at f/5.6, Av, 16 frames in three rounds. Every patch and restore exited 0, and the photo list grew by 16. The frames took 98 s from first to last, about 6.1 s each (Read, log uptimes).

  • All seven slots work. The lens positions (maker note 0x0240) fall frame by frame from 41937 to 33120, on a line in 1/distance at 2435 per dioptre (Read). That includes the frames sent through the 100–500 cm slots, which had not run before.
  • Frame 10 (0.61 m) is 0.09 D off the line, just beyond the 0.08 D tolerance. The planned overlap is 0.08 D, so that step has about none (Read; cause not known).
  • The slope is twice that of probe 2 (about 1200 per dioptre), and the absolute values differ again. The scale of the position word may depend on the lens state or the aperture: the probes ran in P mode, this run at f/5.6 (Inferred, not settled). check fits the line per run, so this does not matter for the check.
  • Seen in the photos: the blind at about 0.9 m is crisp in frame 12, and the window and the view outside are sharper in frame 16. The scene had little between 0.25 and 0.7 m, so frames 3–11 are rarely the sharpest anywhere; a scene with something at every depth is still needed to judge the overlap.

6b. Started in the camera: the self-timer watcher (never run; replaced by §8)

tools/focus/focus_watch.py card writes a recipe card with a WATCH.SH. REMOTE.SH starts it at every power-on, unless a solo run is armed, and copies its files to RAM while the card is still Linux's alone. After that the watcher only reads RAM and asks webapid; its log lines go through PULL.SH like every other solo line.

Using it: tripod, Av at the aperture you want, focus mode Snap with the snap distance set to the nearest thing that must be sharp, self-timer 2 s or 10 s. Press the shutter. The self-timer frame is an ordinary photo; once it is saved the AF lamp comes on and the camera brackets from that distance to infinity. At the end the snap distance, Snap and the self-timer are set back and the lamp stays on for 3 s. With the self-timer off, or any other focus mode, nothing happens.

What it reads (Read, 1.60):

It reads them once a second with PEEK.BIN (read-only /dev/mem, word-aligned, RTOS range only). While Snap, a distance and a self-timer are set, it counts the files in /v1/photos; one more starts the bracket. The plan comes from the snap distance and the aperture in /v1/props (av), rounded to the next wider of f/2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11 and 16, so the frames overlap at least as planned. In P mode probe 2 logged av empty; without a value the f/2.8 plan is used (most frames). All 42 plans (7 distances × 6 apertures, focus_watch.py plans) are prepared on the Mac as patch files, as in §6.

Not yet confirmed in the camera (Inferred; the first log shows it as watch focus F snap C timer T): that the menus store the same three values as UserData Set, that the object sits at the same address after every power-on, and that av holds the aperture in Av mode. If the pointer does not look like RAM, the watcher does nothing.

8. Focus modes on the dial (probe 3 run; watcher built, not yet run)

The self-timer watcher in §6b gives way to two modes on the mode dial, U1 and U2 by default (chosen 2026-11-25; real menu entries would need a flash and are left for later):

  • Macro stack. Focus on the nearest point, then on the farthest; the camera shoots the frames between them, spaced for the aperture.
  • Snap meter. Focus on a subject; the camera goes to a fixed focus at that distance, or at the hyperfocal distance when the subject is beyond it.

A street bracket (snap distance ±2 frames) is dropped for now: from the card each frame takes 1.5–3 s.

The mode watcher (first run failed, fixed, not yet rerun)

First run, 2026-11-25: the dial and the lens distance were read (U2 metered 1887 and 12164 mm), but every patch file failed its readback, so no lens moved. The camera's BusyBox 1.24.1 dd knows only bs count seek skip if of; conv=notrunc worked on the Mac only. The file is now rebuilt from two dd reads around the new word. Also, that BusyBox's hexdump -C prints only * for an all-zero first line, so every zero word read as missing. That includes the copyright word, which is empty (probe 3 part D's "none" and the watcher's "unread"). hexdump -v -C fixes it. The tests' mock BusyBox now rejects unknown dd operands and reproduces the hexdump bug, and every applet used must be in the camera's applet list. U1 was still on Snap 30 cm (near 304, far 281 mm), so the macro mode now refuses Snap, MF and Infinity and logs it.

Second run: the files were built and the tag switched on. Both modes read AF distances (U1 near 1821 and far 684 mm; U2 652 mm). But the camera would not run the rebuilt file: "Permission denied", exit 126. The new file from >$1.t has no execute bit. It is now set with chmod 755 before the mv, and the test mocks run a patch file only when it is executable.

Third run (2026-11-26): U1 worked.

  • AF gave near 863 and far 388 mm. Twelve frames were planned (401 … ~860 mm), and frames 1–9 were shot about every 8.2 s. Each carries GRFS:7308:<i>/12:<mm> in EXIF Copyright.
  • Frame 1 (401 mm) is sharp on the keyboard, frame 9 (632 mm) further back, so the slot patches moved the lens as planned. The maker-note position reads ~8200 on all of them, including the AF photos, so it is no use here.
  • The screen shows the stock labels of the rewritten slots (0.3, 1, 1.5 m …). That looked wrong, so the camera was switched off at frame 9. This left the tag in the camera, with attach On; the next start read "copyright set, attach 0".
  • U2 metered 829 mm and switched to Snap, but from Snap a half-press no longer focuses, so it could not measure again.

Changes:

  • Tag marker. Before the tag goes in, the watcher asks for WATCH/TAG, holding the original attach byte. The recipe card's PULL.SH writes it to the card; PULL.SH is still the only Linux writer while the camera runs. untag asks for it to be removed. The boot copies it to RAM, and a start that finds it clears the copyright and sets attach back. card --clear-tag Off plants it, to clear this run's leftover.
  • CPTAG once per bracket instead of twice per frame. The log now gives the bracket's time.
  • Lens retry. The first macro photo after power-on read no lens distance twice, so the read is retried for 2 s. If it still fails, the log names the empty pointer level.
  • U2 = AF-S + Full Press Snap (StillFullPressSnap). Every settled new lens distance re-meters the 30 cm slot. A reading within 2 % of the snap distance is ignored: that is where a full-press shot leaves the lens. The meter no longer switches the focus mode.
  • Dial changes: each mode recalls its own settings (probe 3: P 250 cm, U1 30 cm). So when the dial has moved, the watcher sends no settings back and only undoes its RAM patches and the tag.

Fourth run (2026-11-26, U2 only): the meter measured again on every half-press (1.7, 1.7, 1.9, 2.1, 6.0 m, hyperfocal) with every patch at rc 0. The user read the snap distance as stuck at 0.3 m. The Full Press Snap photo R0007319 (EXIF focus mode Snap) is not focused at 0.3 m, though: the objects at 0.3–0.4 m are badly blurred, and a folding rule at about 1.5–2 m is nearly sharp. The 0.3 m was the screen label of the borrowed 30 cm setting. This fits the firmware: ApplySnapDistance (0x532af3d8) only acts in Snap and Infinity, and a Full Press Snap shot (shutter path 0x5323a578, event 0x7b instead of 0x7a) is recorded as focus mode Snap. The new meter shot log line will confirm it. One garbage reading (536870893 mm) right after turning to U2 metered the hyperfocal distance. Changes: the meter borrows the setting nearest to the distance, lens readings of 1000 m and more are ignored, and each photo logs the lens position. This run was on the GR Mod dim-light AF build; the multipatch checks of the stock words passed (rc 0).

Decision after run 4: U2 switches the camera into real Snap after the measurement, as in the second run. To measure again, use a stock key function: the custom key options (CustomSwitchFn handler 0x5332e6ec) include ToggleAfSnap, so the Fn button is set to AF/Snap toggle. Fn gives AF-S with the lens still at the snap distance. The watcher takes that reading as the base (meter remeasure) and meters once the lens has moved and holds for two readings. That includes AF settling back on the same distance after hunting. In Snap itself the lens is never measured. Full Press Snap is no longer used.

Fifth run (2026-11-26): with upper-case template names (Ts0.BIN was not found on the camera: every meter logged build failed) the meter switched to Snap at the measured distance, and photo R0007321 logged lens 5618 mm at snap 5618 mm. A new aperture recomputed it each time. Fn did nothing: it had been set to AF/Snap toggle in another mode, and U2 recalls its own button setup. Fn is stored at settings +0x922 (3 = ToggleAfSnap); the watcher logs it on entering U2 (meter fn 3).

Sixth run: with Fn on AF/Snap toggle in U2 (log meter fn 3), Fn still did nothing. Reason (Read): the toggle switches the focus mode in use, settings +0xc6 (setter 0x532b03b4, read by 0x5326c5f8, which ApplySnapDistance tests), between Snap and the focus setting +0x11c. The watcher had set the setting itself to Snap, so the toggle had no AF mode to return to. Now the watcher leaves the setting at AF-S: it sets the snap distance and presses Fn itself through the remote key SW_fn (next to SW_sws and SW_swr in the debug command table). It reads +0xc6 to check that Snap came: if not within three polls, it presses again (a press while the shutter is still half down may be ignored), three times at most. The user presses Fn to go back to AF. Without the toggle on Fn the watcher sets the focus mode to Snap as before.

Seventh run: the watcher's Fn press (log meter fn press) did bring Snap (meter in snap), but afterwards the physical Fn no longer went back to AF. Before it, the user's own Fn presses had toggled both ways. The remote key handlers (SW_fn, like SW_sws) write a separate virtual key state (0x536a7474), which may keep overriding the button. Replaced: the focus mode in use has its own remote setting, CommonTempFocus (handler 0x532dd620, values AutoArea … Snap, Infinity, None = follow the setting), which calls the +0xc6 setter and applies the focus (ApplySnapDistance). The meter now sends the snap setting, CommonTempFocus Snap and its three blinks as one relay batch. Leaving U2 with Snap still in use while the new mode's setting is not Snap sends CommonTempFocus None. Speed: the run took 4–8 s from AF to Snap, because every round made two web requests (props and photos, about 2 s each) and the meter used five relay batches (about 2 s each). Now on U2 the lens is read every round (about 0.5 s), props only every fourth round, and the photo list not at all; a measurement needs three equal readings (about 1 s at rest).

Eighth run: CommonTempFocus Snap worked (log meter focus 10 4 s after the measurement). Fn then put the AF-C icon on the screen, but the camera stayed in Snap with no focus frame, and the focus mode in use (+0xc6) stayed 10. The toggle keeps its own state below the temporary focus. Probe: while the meter's Snap is in use, the watcher compares the settings block (+0 to +0xb00) with the snapshot taken when Snap came and logs each changed word once (meter probe off:old>new). If one of the words read by the in-use focus functions (+0x44, +0x110, +0x11c, +0xa3c, +0xa40) changes, it sends CommonTempFocus None (meter fn seen).

Ninth run: the probe found the toggle. Fn changed only the word at +0x11c: 000d0001>0001000a, i.e. the focus setting +0x11c went from Select (1) to Snap (10), and +0x11e, empty (13) before, now holds Select. Pressed again, it swaps back. So AF/Snap toggle swaps the focus setting with a back-up slot, which has its own remote setting StillBackUpFocus (handler 0x532ddf84, setter 0x53242618). When the watcher set StillFocus Snap alone, the slot was empty and Fn had nothing to return to. The temporary focus of the eighth run sat on top of the toggle, so the two fought ("sometimes it worked"). Now the meter makes exactly the toggle's state: StillBackUpFocus <AF mode>, then StillFocus Snap. The probe, CommonTempFocus and the blinks are gone; the batch starts with AUD_beep (the AF beep, debug command 0x5302257c) as feedback. Snap came 4–5 s after each measurement (relay plus card sync). One measurement read 1761 mm where the user had focused at about 5 m; the log now lists the readings on the way (path).

Tenth run: Fn and Snap worked (six measurements, including 6.2 m). Two faults. (1) Snap came back without a half-press: in P the aperture follows the light, and every change re-metered and sent Snap, even in AF. Now a new aperture counts only while in Snap, and only when it moves the snap distance (subject beyond the hyperfocal distance); then only the distance is sent, without a beep. (2) The first half-press after turning the dial to U2 was missed: the dial is noticed at the next poll (2–3 s), and the first lens reading in U2 (the base) was already the subject. Now the last lens reading in the previous mode is the base.

Eleventh run: works (14 measurements from 0.2 m to infinity). Three batches in a row were acked but the focus stayed AF; the user was half-pressing again around that time. Now Snap is sent once more if the focus is still AF 5 s after the batch, twice at most (meter snap retry, meter snap failed); Snap that came and went is Fn and is left alone. The development log lines are gone (lens path, focus in use, remeasure, file list, Fn code); the watcher logs the Fn code only when it is not AF/Snap toggle.

Exact distance on screen and in the photo (next run): the snap label is text. The setting's cm maps to a label id (0x53291ec4: 30 → 0x3e, 40 → 0x3f, 100 → 0x40, 150 → 0x41, 200 → 0x42, 250 → 0x43, 350 → 0x44, 500 → 0x45), the widget (setter 0x53396f80, which skips an unchanged id) resolves it via [[0x55002bac + 4·language] + 4·id] to an entry {u16 length with NUL, u16 1, pointer to UTF-16} (0x5339666c). The language is the byte at +0x9ba (getter 0x532465dc, index switch 0x533963f8). The four-character labels (0.3m, 1.5m, 2.5m, 3.5m) sit in 12 bytes, so five characters fit with the length 5 → 6. The meter now uses 1.5, 2.5 or 3.5 m (not 0.3 m, which 0x532af020 can show as 0.4 m), never the one in use, so the new id redraws. It writes the text (0.48m, 12.2m) with LB2–LB4.BIN (only the words that change), and restores it with the mm patch. The patch tool checks every stock word first. If the chain does not check out it logs meter label off lang N and falls back to the nearest stock setting. With the camera's copyright empty, the Snap batch also sets GRSNAP:<snap mm>:<subject mm> as the copyright (attached), using CPTAG.BIN as the U1 bracket does. Fn back to AF and leaving the mode clear it in one batch (CPEMPTY.BIN).

Twelfth run: the label patch wrote (exit 0) but the screen kept "2.5m"/"3.5m", and the meter's choice of 1.5/2.5/3.5 m instead of the nearest setting put the labels far off. Cause: for the snap ids (0x3e–0x45, 0xc) the widget setter 0x53396f80 forces language 0x14 (0x53396f4c: +0x10c = 1, +0x10d = 0x14), and 0x533963f8 maps 0x14 to table index 0. So the label always comes from [0x55002bac], not from the table of the language that is set. Neither the size code (0x5339666c, cached only while +0xf3 is clear) nor the draw (0x533970a4) caches the text. Now: table index 0, and the nearest setting again. The text goes in only when the nearest setting is 1.5, 2.5 or 3.5 m. When that setting is already in use, 5 m is sent first so that the widget sees a new id. The tag then goes in its own batch, because both would not fit the 512-byte relay batch.

Thirteenth run: one measurement landed on 1.5 m and was patched to 1.60m. The user saw "1.6", so the label draws, perhaps cut to the stock width. The six others landed on settings without room (0.3, 1, 2 m). Now every measurement uses the nearest of the four settings with four-character labels (0.3, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 m; for 0.3 m both ids 0x3e and 0x3f). The text is at most four characters: under 1 m 48cm, up to 10 m 1.6m, beyond that 12m (length word 5 → 4). Only words that change are written, 1 to 6 of them (LB1–LB6.BIN). Photo tag confirmed: R0007322–24 carry GRSNAP:346:346, GRSNAP:1089:1089, GRSNAP:1730:1730, focus mode Snap.

Fourteenth run: labels shown as measured (1.6m, 98cm, 56cm, 1.8m, 9.3m). Twice the camera dropped the Snap batch, and the retry brought Snap about 9 s after the measurement, which felt like Snap without a half-press. The watcher measures while the half-press is still held (lens still for about 1 s), and Snap sent then seems to be ignored. The half-press switch: the static SwitchMonitorFactory at 0x5536c604 (initialiser 0x536a3edc) holds the SwitchMonitor at +4 (0x5536c608, allocated by 0x536a4040). Its init 0x536a439c builds the S1 CpuSwitch (ctor 0x536a6030; key code 0, event 0x21600201) at +0x5c (0x536a4574), and S2 at +0x60 (event 0x2160021c, key code 1). The scan 0x536a5c38 keeps the last state at +4 and the current one at +8, 1 while pressed (active-low line). A remote SW_ override, once used, masks the button until the next power cycle. The meter checks the class pointer 0x53d5b9c0, the event id and the key code once per visit (meter s1 ok or off). It then waits for the release before the Snap batch, up to 15 s (meter s1 held N, in 0.1 s polls). The beep is gone at the user's request; the Snap label with the exact distance is the feedback.

Fifteenth run: meter s1 ok (the checks held), but no wait was ever logged, so the word read 0 at every measurement. Without the beep the user held the half-press longer, and almost every first batch was dropped. The retries sent only StillBackUpFocus + StillFocus Snap, so Snap came at the old setting: stock distance and label, "reset to a previous one". Now each retry also sends StillSnapDistanceCm. The probe logs the S1 words (+4, +8) and S2 +8 each time they change (meter s1 words).

Sixteenth run: the first half-press after power-on did nothing: the watcher only started its loop at 20 s, after clearing a leftover tag in two batches (the relay's first acknowledgement came at 17 s). The subject at 1066 mm was logged and its batches acknowledged three times, but focus never read Snap, and 541 mm came only with the first retry. After the 893 mm Snap, focus read Snap at 36.6 s and AF again at 37.05 s. The S1 word read 0 0 0 except once. Why it was slow and unsure: every lens reading was PEEK.BIN | hexdump | awk per pointer (30+ processes a round), each round slept 0.3 s, and every fourth round waited about 2 s for /v1/props. A subject was logged 2 to 5 s after focus, the batch was acknowledged 2.5 to 3 s later, and focus was looked at about once a second, so a Snap that came and went within a second was never seen. A press that left the lens where it was, or came while a batch or the props request blocked the loop, was missed. Firmware (Read, 1.60): the remote StillFocus handler 0x532ddd7c calls the setter 0x532730f8. The setter stores settings +0x11c first, then sends synchronous requests to the lens/capture controller (0x53273084 → 0x5354b564, mutex and wait, both without a timeout), then posts UI event 0x177. The Fn toggle uses the same setter: 0x532b0b0c saves the focus mode to the back-up +0x11e, and 0x532b0b6c sets focus = back-up, then back-up = 13. StillSnapDistanceCm (0x53242680) and StillBackUpFocus (0x53242618) are plain stores. The suspected reset 0x5326cc40 (via 0x5327a058) is a menu dialog action, not a revert. The S1 state comes from a sub-CPU snapshot in the monitor (+0xd0: word +4, byte +8, copied by 0x536a5100 in the monitor loop 0x536a51ac). Rebuilt for run 17: SAMP.BIN (af_debug_card.SAMP_S, tested in the unicorn ARM emulator) samples the lens and the focus word every 5 ms in one process and returns when the lens has moved and stood still 250 ms, when the focus byte changes, or after 1 s. /v1/props runs in the background. The Snap batch sends the plain stores first (snap cm, back-up = the AF mode, 150 ms each), then StillFocus Snap (300 ms). After the acknowledgement the watcher reads focus, back-up and cm back (meter set N focus F backup B cm C) and sends only what is missing, three batches at most. The tag follows in its own batch. In Snap it waits on the focus byte: back to AF with the back-up 13 is Fn; with the back-up still the AF mode within 5 s it is the camera, and Snap goes again (meter snap lost). The leftover tag and the tag-off after Fn wait for two quiet rounds, so they no longer stand between a press and the loop. On entering U2 the watcher logs lens, focus, back-up, cm, +0x108 and S1 (meter entry); S1 held there makes the settled lens a subject. A tracer (SAMP.BIN t) records each change of the lens (every 50 ms), the S1/S2 words, the sub-CPU snapshot, settings +0x11c, +0x130, +0xc4, +0x108 and the AF action word. Records are drained into the log while idle (tr, decoded by focus_watch.py log), 200 per tracer, six tracers per visit. If SAMP.BIN is missing or fails, the same waits run through PEEK.BIN (meter samp off).

Seventeenth run: samp 1; all seven Snaps were in place after the first batch (meter set 1 focus 10), 1.2 to 1.4 s after the subject. The tracer showed three things. The half-press switch words (S1 +4/+8, S2 +8, the sub-CPU snapshot) never changed at 200 Hz through every press, so they are not used. The contrast AF action word (CAF object +0xc44c) runs 1 → 2 → 4 (→ 2 → 4) → 8 or 0x40 → 0x80 → 1 on each AF, and the lens reaches its end within 50 ms of the 1. The lens reads 1000000 from power-on until the first AF. The lens alone gave the subject 0.7 to 2.6 s after AF; at power-on it was caught only by a 4 mm drift. The "snap lost" was Fn: Fn writes the focus mode first and the back-up 13 about 10 ms later, and the watcher had read in between and sent Snap again. A half-press during the Snap batch (55.5 s, AF on a subject near 32 cm) was overwritten when the batch's Snap of the subject before moved the lens back to 2.5 m: the "previous distance". Now an AF run (the action word leaving 1) starts the measurement, and the lens 120 ms after the word returns to 1 is the subject, also when it lands at the same distance as before. During every batch the same watch runs in the background, and a newer subject is measured right after (meter pressed during batch). A back-up other than 13 is read again after 100 ms. Entering U2 within 40 s of power-on with a valid lens counts as an AF done before the watcher looked. The S1 code is gone; the tracer keeps lens, focus, cm, focus in use, +0x108 and the action word.

Eighteenth run (2026-11-27, two power-ons, no photos): the action word marked every AF run (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 0x80 → 1, 0.2 to 0.6 s), and eight subjects from 38 cm to 1.8 m went to Snap. The new paths all worked: a half-press before the watcher started (1805 mm, "subject before the start"), Fn back to AF with no "snap lost", and two presses during a Snap batch (992 and 382 mm, measured next). Two presses were lost (34.9 s and 57.1 s): Fn and a half-press came about 2 s after Snap, while the GRSNAP tag batch was still running. The background watch saw the AF start, but the AF ended (35.05, 57.66 s) 120 ms before the batch did (35.17, 57.78 s), and afbgend killed the watch 30 ms after the batch, inside its 120 ms lens check. Now the watch marks an AF run it has seen start (af.run), and the end of the batch waits for it, up to 3 s (meter pressed during batch <mm> waited <n x 50 ms>).

Nineteenth run (2026-11-27, one power-on, fast half-press and release cycles on purpose, about one every 3 to 4 s): 19 subjects from 44 cm to 1.8 m, each pressed while the batch of the one before was still out, each measured next, so the newest subject always won; Snap landed only in the pauses. The wait for an AF run under way was needed three times (waited 5, 250 ms). No press was missed. Still open: no watch runs in the 0.8 to 1.5 s between a measured press and its first batch (patch and label), and the tracer drains only while idle, so it recorded nothing during the series.

tools/focus/focus_watch.py card --macro U1 --meter U2. REMOTE.SH starts WATCH.SH at every power-on.

  • Macro stack: take a photo on the nearest point (one blink), then on the farthest (two blinks). The live lens distance at each photo gives near and far. The frames between them are spaced in dioptres for the aperture, as in §6, with at least 5 and at most 35. Afterwards the focus mode and snap distance are set back. The dial is checked after each round; no second photo within 60 s forgets the near point.
  • Snap meter: in AF modes, once the lens has moved and stood still for 250 ms (SAMP.BIN), the camera is switched to Snap at that distance, or at the hyperfocal distance when the subject lies beyond it, and the result is read back in RAM. Fn (AF/Snap toggle) and a half-press measure again. The setting with a four-character label nearest to it in dioptres (0.3, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 m) is rewritten in RAM to the exact distance and label (single-slot templates TS0–TS6.BIN, label patches LB1–LB6.BIN). It stays so while the dial is on the meter's U mode; leaving it puts the stock words back.
  • Millimetres in the camera: the near and far points are only known in the camera, so the card holds multipatch templates T1–T7.BIN with placeholder words. The shell writes each movw r1, #mm with printf and dd and reads it back before running the file. On the Mac the result equals the files of §6 byte for byte (tests).
  • Tag: GRFS:<far photo number>:<frame>/<frames>:<mm> in EXIF Copyright, only when the copyright reads empty; empty again afterwards.
