Image effects: grain and the digital-filter stage (GR IIIx 1.60)

Question: can the firmware get more image settings, such as a grittier grain, next to Hue, Sharpness and Contrast, and can they work while shooting? Offline analysis of the decoded 1.60 image, 2026-11-25. Nothing has been run on a camera. Labels: Read, Structural, Inferred, as in the other documents. Every Read address below is checked by tools/effects_map.py.

Short answer

  • Grain ("Particle") is a complete effect in the camera. Four existing settings run through one post-processing stage: Shading, Clarity, Grain and HDR strength. The grain code does not ask which image control is active (Read).
  • Three gates keep grain off the colour controls:
    • a capability table in RAW development;
    • the settings store when shooting;
    • a planner that decides per capture whether the stage runs at all (Read).
  • Grain strength is one immediate value per level (High 0x100, Mid 0x80, Low 0x40). A grittier grain is therefore a three-word change (Read; how it looks is untested).
  • New effects in the strict sense (halation, colour grain, grain size) have no ready knob. They would need new image code (§5).

2. How the grain is made (Read, labels Inferred)

  • 0x5367ad3c turns the level into a scale, read in 1/256 units: High 0x100 (1.0), Mid 0x80, Low 0x40.
  • It copies three 256-entry signed tables:
    • 0x538f19d4: a brightening curve, up to +33 levels at mid-grey;
    • 0x538f1bd4: a darkening curve, down to −33;
    • 0x538f1dd4: an S-curve, ±51.
  • From the first two it builds two LUTs: out[i] = i + t[0] + ((t[i] − i − t[0]) · scale >> 8). High uses the full curve, Low a quarter of it.
  • Library calls then build noise planes (0x536789f0 with 2 and 3, 0x53678b7c with 64 × 64) and filter them (8-tap). Finally they composite the up and down images through the noise mask with the S-curve (mode 5, 0x5376cd10).
  • The result is luminance grain whose amplitude peaks in the mid-tones (Inferred from the curves). It already runs on a colour image: Negative Film has grain (Read).

5. What is not there

  • Grain size: the noise planes are built with constants (64 × 64 in 0x53678b7c, 0xa0d0 in 0x53679180, an 8-tap filter). Which of them sets the apparent grain size is not decoded (Structural). Changing them might change the grain size or break the routine. A RAM test can tell.
  • Colour grain: the stage composites one mask over the image. No per-channel amplitude was found. The SetNoiseCharacteristicBoundaryR/G/B calls belong to the ElaDriver noise hardware (0x5375d8c8…), not to this stage (Structural).
  • Halation, bloom, a vignette amount beyond Shading ±4: no such routine was found. These would be new image code running on the ARM cores on a 24 MP frame. That is a separate study, not a patch.
  • A new menu row: the image-control menu takes its rows from its own tables, which are not decoded. Until then, new values are set by the card (UserData Set or a card file), not in the menu.

5a. RAM test card (arms A and B)

tools/effects_card.py writes work/effects-card/. Nothing has been run on a camera yet. The card needs SD scripts already turned on (README, setup 1). It has nothing else on it: no Wi-Fi bench, no relay, no AF words.

  • At power-on, SCRIPT/STARTUP.TTL waits for Linux (MARK.SH, as on the other cards). It then runs SCRIPT/EFFECTS/APPLY.SH once.
  • APPLY.SH runs A.BIN and/or B.BIN, for each arm whose .ON file is on the card. These are the /dev/mem multi-word writers of af_debug_card.multipatch_bin.
    • Each writer checks the build identity at 0x53002038, then that every site holds its stock word, before it writes anything.
    • It reads every word back afterwards.
    • The result goes into EFFECTS.LOG as one line per power-on: uptime, then A=0 B=0. Exit 0 means written, 6 means already in place, 3 another build, 5 an unexpected word (nothing written), 7 a read-back mismatch.
  • The AF lamp signals the result: it flashes twice when every armed patch is in place, and stays on for 3 s when a writer failed or Linux did not answer within 60 s.
  • Linux writes the card once per power-on, after the script has stopped polling: one log line, then sync. This avoids the stalls the recipe relay had when Linux wrote the card while the RTOS was reading it (remote-control doc).
  • Arm B uses the scales 0x100 / 0x200 / 0x300 by default: Low becomes the stock High, and Mid and High go two and three times further. Other values: --grain 0x80,0x180,0x280. --grain 0x40,0x80,0x100 (stock) leaves arm B off the card.

Automated (arm B): tools/effects_solo.py card writes a recipe card (tools/recipe_card.py) with one solo run. After a single power-on, the camera works through it by itself:

  • stock grain on Monotone (Off, Low, Mid, High) and Negative Film (Off, High);
  • B.BIN, then the same at the patched scales;
  • B.BIN restore, then one more stock High as a check;
  • finally A.BIN, left in RAM for the RAW-development check by hand.

Every patch step first waits 6 s, so that the last photo has finished developing. effects_solo.py import copies the photos. It measures the fine-detail spread of a 1024 px centre crop, compares patched Low with stock High (the same scale, 0x100), and looks for the maker-note slot that records grain.

Test by hand, one change at a time. Take each comparison twice, with and without the card:

  1. B while shooting, no RAW development needed: Monotone, grain High, a mid-grey wall or sky. Then grain Low with the card, which should look like stock High.
  2. A in RAW development: playback → RAW development of a DNG → Positive Film → parameters.
    • Is there a Grain row? (Not settled offline: both RAW-development routines for slot 15 check the table, but the screen controller may draw its rows from its own list.)
    • If there is: develop with grain High and compare with Negative Film grain High.
  3. A + B: Positive Film, grain High in RAW development.
  4. Switch off, take the card out, and switch on again: grain must be back to stock. Repeat a few power cycles with the card in, and check that EFFECTS.LOG has one line per power-on.

Result of the automated run, 2026-11-25 (arm B)

The solo run R0007217–R0007227 ran 11 shots with the default scales 0x100 / 0x200 / 0x300. All three writer calls exited 0 (patch B, restore B, patch A), and every photo's image control matched the plan. The table gives the standard deviation of the fine detail (luma minus a 4 px Gaussian blur) in a 1024 px centre crop, on a lit curtain at ISO 200 (Read from the photos):

ShotGrain measure
Monotone stock Off / Low / Mid / High7.7 / 8.8 / 10.2 / 14.0
Negative Film stock Off / High7.4 / 14.2
Monotone with B: Low / Mid / High14.0 / 23.2 / 32.8
Negative Film with B: High33.9
Monotone High after B.BIN restore14.0
  • Patched Low uses the stock High scale (0x100) and measures the same: 14.04 against 14.04. After the restore, stock High measures the same again. This confirms the scale reading of §2 and the clean restore.
  • Clipping is small: 0.06 % of the pixels are pure white at 0x300, and none at 0x200.
  • The grain stays fine (pixel scale). Only its amplitude grows, and it remains weaker in the shadows.
  • To the eye, 0x200 reads as a gritty film grain and 0x300 as harsh (Inferred).

Maker notes (Read): Pentax tag 0x0247 slot 19 records the grain of the monotone controls as Off 0, Low 5, Mid 3, High 1. It does not change with arm B, because the stored setting is unchanged. For Negative Film, slots 17 and 19 read 8 at both grain Off and High, so Negative Film's grain is not recorded in this tag.

Arm A on the camera, 2026-11-25: no Grain row

With the six capability words in RAM (EFFECTS.LOG: A=0), RAW development of a Positive Film shot showed no Grain row. The capability table is only the setter's check; the rows come from the row lists above (Read). Arm A now also writes 26 row-list words: row 15 goes into the zero padding after each colour list, and the count grows by one. Standard, Vivid and both films get the row in all three variants. Bleach Bypass, Retro and Cross Process get it in variant 0 only, because their variant 1 and 2 lists already have 11 rows, and a 12th could exceed the scroll list if it was sized before the card ran. HDR Tone and the monotone lists stay stock (tools/tests/test_effects.py).

Second try, same day: with the 32 words in RAM the Grain row appears for Positive Film. The JPEG developed from R0007217 (R0007228, Positive Film, saturation +2, key +1, contrast +3, sharpness +1) shows no grain at 1:1. The noise in a flat patch is 6.1, against 4.3 for Negative Film without grain and 8.2 with stock High, and the higher contrast and sharpness may explain the 6.1 alone. Likely cause (Inferred): RAW development goes through the same still planner, which runs the digital-filter stage for the colour tones only when shading or clarity is set (§3), so the grain value is stored but never applied. Check without a patch: develop again with grain High and clarity +1. If grain then shows, the planner is the gate, and C1 (§4) lifts it.

Third gate: ImageTuning writes grain back as 0 (Read)

Two more developments of R0007217 as Positive Film with Clarity +1, one with Grain High (R0007229) and one with Grain Off (R0007230), are pixel for pixel the same. So the planner (§3) is not the only gate. Everything from the screen to the filter passes grain for any control:

  • the row setter 0x5328843c and the dispatcher 0x53288390 check the capability table (arm A);
  • the property check 0x535542cc rejects grain only when +0x6c0 is 2, whatever the control;
  • SetEffectParameter and the effect routine 0x53680dbc apply any level from 1 to 3.

The gate is ImageTuning.cpp 0x536db7dc. It switches on the image tone +0x77c, fills a tuning block per tone, and its shared tail 0x536db9c0 stores shading, clarity, grain and HDR strength back into the property. The monotone and Negative Film branches load grain from +0x795 (0x536dc0b8). The colour branches (tones 0x0b, 0x0c/0x11, 0x12, 0x13, 0x15) hand the tail a register they set to 0, so grain is erased before the filter reads it. Arm A now also replaces those five mov r6, rX with Negative Film's ldrb r6, [r3, #0x795]. In every branch r3 still holds the property there, and r6 is used only by the tail.

ImageTuning also runs when shooting. While A is in RAM, a colour shot with shading or clarity set may therefore keep a grain byte left over from an earlier monotone or Negative Film setting (Inferred, not tested).

Grain on Positive Film, 2026-11-25

With the 37 words in RAM, R0007217 developed as Positive Film with Grain High (Read from the JPEGs, centre crop):

DevelopmentGrain measureFlat-area noise

Without clarity there is no grain: RAW development goes through the still planner too, which for colour tones runs the filter stage only when shading or clarity is off neutral (raw value 4). Arm A therefore now also carries C1, the three planner branches (40 words in all).

6. Open, to settle on the camera

  1. Arm A on the RAM card (§5a): does the RAW-development screen show a Grain row for Positive Film, and does grain look right on a colour image?
  2. Arm B: what 0x200 and 0x300 look like, and whether they clip.
  3. Arm C: what property +0x795 holds after switching from Monotone with grain to Positive Film. Only the 15 Particle setters send id 0xc3 (Read), so the stale value may stay. The planner then skips the stage unless shading or clarity is set. If the value does stay, C1 alone would already carry the grain of the last monotone setting into colour captures.
  4. Where C2's hook goes: the code that copies the settings into a capture's CommonProperty (StillControl/src/BEStillCommand.cpp initialises +0x793…+0x796 at 0x5301f3e0). The per-capture fill is not yet traced (Structural).
