Faster autofocus in dim light on the GR IIIx

What we found: a stable pair of settings that makes the GR IIIx focus faster in dim light. AF took 23–39 % less time wherever the camera dropped to its slowest measuring rate, in 3 scene and light combinations on two days. In good light it was no slower, and it did not fail or focus wrongly more often than stock.

The two changes: the camera accepts a sharpness peak with a somewhat lower safety margin, and in the darkest mode it measures 49 instead of 29 frames per second. Neither helped on its own; together they cut the slowest part of a press, the focus sweep, by about a third. The patch is experimental: tested from the camera's memory on one camera and firmware, then flashed into that camera's firmware.

The Ricoh GR IIIx focuses by contrast: it moves the lens and looks for the sharpest point. In dim light that gets slow. This page follows one question through the measurements: where does the time go, and can a small firmware change win some of it back without making focus less reliable? All presses were made on one camera (firmware 1.60), most of them remote-controlled over Wi-Fi so that stock and patched settings could be interleaved blind.

Words used on this page
Contrast AF
The GR moves the lens through a range and measures how sharp (how contrasty) the focus area is at each step. The sharpest step is the focus.
Sweep
That run of the lens with a measurement per frame.
fps
Frames per second the camera measures during a sweep. In good light 200; in dim light it drops to 100, 49 and finally 29 to collect more light per frame.
Margin
How far the sharpness peak must stand out before the camera accepts it. It is raised in dim light so noise is not taken for a peak.
Patched
The combined change tested here: a lower margin (found by the optimizer) plus 49 instead of 29 fps in the darkest mode. Tested from the camera's memory; since 2026-11-20 flashed into the test camera.
Locked / ended / failed
Locked: the camera confirmed a peak (green frame). Ended: it stopped without confirming one. Failed: it gave up.
ND filter
The GR's built-in grey filter, switched in remotely to make the same scene darker.
About the data

Drawn from the raw logs, not copied: 1,990 debug CSV dumps (unique finished sweeps, as in tools/af_csv.py), 9 state traces (80 traced presses with brightness data) and the remote A/B sessions under work/af-logs. The lab notebook with every run is AF debug log on the camera. Hover a mark for its values; each chart has its numbers under Data. Regenerate with python3 tools/af_charts.py.

1. Why dim light makes AF slow

Four steps: what the camera measures, what it does when light falls, what that costs, and where in a press the time ends up.

2. Two levers, tried one at a time

Two settings decide dim-light AF: the margin (how sure the camera must be) and the frame rate (how fast it measures). Each was changed on its own first.

Lowering the margin

A lower margin lets the camera lock in darker light. Cut too far, it locks on nothing.

3. Letting an optimizer search the combination

Since neither lever worked alone, an optimizer (Optuna) tried combinations: margin settings, sweep step and frame rate together. The camera was driven over Wi-Fi, each setting uploaded to its memory and scored on a batch of half-presses, with stock controls in between.

What mattered

The margin settings (factor kb and rise floor) and the frame rate did most of the work.

4. The result: patched against stock

The best combination: a lower margin (exponent 0.875, kb 0.32, falls 2, rise 0.014) and 49 instead of 29 fps in the darkest mode. Tested blind against stock in several scenes and light levels.

5. Limits and what is still open

A faster AF is only useful if it does not focus on the wrong thing more often.

Where AF struggles

Without a clear target both settings fail most of the time, and the patch does not claim focus on nothing more often.

Still open

  • Sharpness of real photos at the patched focus positions.
  • A blind handheld test.
  • A low-contrast target in 29 fps light.
  • One camera and one firmware (1.60); other bodies may differ.
  • The tests patched the camera's memory, gone after a restart; the setting is now flashed into the test camera only. It is a research result, not a released firmware update.
